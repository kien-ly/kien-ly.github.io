# Behavioral Interview Prep

> Behavioral interviews test how you handle real situations. FAANG uses these to assess leadership, ownership, and collaboration.

---

## The STAR Method

Every answer should follow this structure:

| Component | What to Include | Time |
|-----------|-----------------|------|
| **S**ituation | Context, team, stakes | 20 sec |
| **T**ask | Your specific responsibility | 10 sec |
| **A**ction | What YOU did (not the team) | 60-90 sec |
| **R**esult | Quantified outcome + learning | 20 sec |

**Total:** 2-3 minutes per answer

---

## Amazon Leadership Principles (Used by Many Companies)

| Principle | What They're Testing | Sample Question |
|-----------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **Ownership** | Taking responsibility beyond your role | "Tell me about a time you went beyond your job description" |
| **Dive Deep** | Getting into details, not just high-level | "Describe a time you had to dig into data to solve a problem" |
| **Bias for Action** | Moving fast with incomplete info | "Tell me about a decision you made without all the data" |
| **Earn Trust** | Building relationships, admitting mistakes | "Describe a time you had to rebuild trust" |
| **Deliver Results** | Achieving outcomes despite obstacles | "Tell me about your most impactful project" |
| **Learn and Be Curious** | Growing, learning from failure | "What's something new you learned recently?" |
| **Insist on Highest Standards** | Not accepting mediocrity | "Tell me about a time you raised the bar" |
| **Think Big** | Long-term vision, ambitious goals | "Describe a time you proposed a big idea" |

## Top 15 Behavioral Questions for Data Engineers

### Leadership & Ownership
1. "Tell me about a time you led a project without formal authority"
2. "Describe a situation where you had to push back on stakeholders"
3. "Tell me about a time you took ownership of something outside your role"

### Conflict & Collaboration
4. "Tell me about a disagreement with a coworker. How did you resolve it?"
5. "Describe a time when you had to work with a difficult stakeholder"
6. "How did you handle a situation where you disagreed with your manager?"

### Failure & Learning
7. "Tell me about your biggest professional failure"
8. "Describe a project that didn't go as planned"
9. "What's the biggest mistake you made in a data pipeline?"

### Problem Solving
10. "Walk me through how you debugged a complex data issue"
11. "Describe a time you had to make a decision with incomplete information"
12. "Tell me about a time you improved a process significantly"

### Communication
13. "How did you explain a technical concept to non-technical stakeholders?"
14. "Describe a time you had to deliver bad news"
15. "How do you handle competing priorities from different stakeholders?"

## Story Bank: Prepare These 5 Stories

One great story can answer multiple questions. Prepare 5 stories that cover different themes:

### Story 1: Technical Leadership
**Covers:** Leadership, ownership, technical decision-making
- Led a migration, chose a technology, drove adoption
- Example: "Led ODI to dbt migration, made architectural decisions"

### Story 2: Conflict Resolution
**Covers:** Disagreement, collaboration, earning trust
- Disagreed with someone, resolved it professionally
- Example: "Pushed back on stakeholder's timeline, found compromise"

### Story 3: Failure → Learning
**Covers:** Mistake, recovery, growth
- Something went wrong, you fixed it, you learned
- Example: "Pipeline bug caused data inconsistency, built validation"

### Story 4: Ambiguity → Action
**Covers:** Incomplete info, bias for action, results
- Unclear requirements, moved forward anyway
- Example: "Started building before specs were final, iterated"

### Story 5: Cross-functional Impact
**Covers:** Communication, stakeholder management, business impact
- Worked with non-engineers, delivered business value
- Example: "Partnered with analysts to improve self-service"

## Sample Answer: "Tell me about a disagreement with a coworker"

### Bad Answer
> "I don't really have disagreements. I'm pretty easy to work with."

*Why it's bad:* Unrealistic, doesn't show conflict resolution skills.

### Good Answer (STAR Format)

**Situation (20 sec):**
> "On my HR Data Factory project, we were migrating from ODI to dbt. Our senior engineer wanted to do a 'big bang' migration—move everything at once. I disagreed."

**Task (10 sec):**
> "As the tech lead, I needed to advocate for my preferred approach while maintaining the relationship."

**Action (90 sec):**
> "I didn't push back in the meeting. Instead, I asked for a follow-up to understand his perspective. His concern was that parallel systems would double operational overhead.
>
> I acknowledged that was valid, then presented data: our last big-bang migration had a 3-week rollback when we found edge cases in production. 
>
> I proposed a middle ground: migrate table-by-table, but with an automated comparison framework that would validate each table before cutover. This addressed his overhead concern while reducing risk.
>
> He agreed to try it on 5 tables. When it worked smoothly, he became the biggest advocate for the approach."

**Result (20 sec):**
> "We completed the migration with zero data incidents. The comparison framework became a standard practice for all future migrations. Key learning: leading with data and finding compromise is more effective than winning arguments."

## Red Flags to Avoid

| Red Flag | What Interviewer Thinks |
|----------|------------------------|
| "We did..." instead of "I did..." | You didn't own it |
| Blaming others | Not accountable |
| No specific numbers/outcomes | Vague, unverifiable |
| Perfect story with no challenges | Not realistic |
| Answer too short (<1 min) | Lack of depth |
| Answer too long (>4 min) | Can't be concise |
| Can't name a failure | Lack of self-awareness |

## Fill-in-the-Blank Templates

### For Leadership Stories
> "I was working on [project] when [challenge arose]. As the [role], I took the initiative to [action]. Specifically, I [detail 1], [detail 2], and [detail 3]. The result was [quantified outcome]. I learned [insight]."

### For Conflict Stories
> "I was collaborating with [person/team] on [project]. We disagreed about [specific issue]. I first [sought to understand], then [proposed solution]. The outcome was [result], and the relationship [improved/was maintained]."

### For Failure Stories
> "On [project], I made the mistake of [specific error]. The impact was [consequence]. I immediately [took ownership by action]. To fix it, I [solution]. To prevent recurrence, I [process improvement]. I learned [insight]."

## Practice Tips

1. **Time yourself:** Each answer should be 2-3 minutes
2. **Practice aloud:** Writing is different from speaking
3. **Record yourself:** Listen for filler words, clarity
4. **Get feedback:** Practice with a friend
5. **Have backups:** If they've heard a story, have another ready
